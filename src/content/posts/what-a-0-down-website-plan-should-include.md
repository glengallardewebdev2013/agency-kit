Pay-monthly websites let a small business get a professional site without a big upfront bill. Plans for small businesses mostly run from about $99 to $350 a month. But they're not all built the same, and the differences show up when you try to change something or leave.

Here's what to check before you sign.

## Who owns the site

This is the big one. Some providers keep the website when you stop paying, so you start again from nothing. Ask directly: who owns the domain, the content and the site itself, and when?

At GallarDesign, your content and domain are yours from day one, and the site is yours after month 12.

## How long the contract runs, and how you get out

A 12-month term is normal, because the first year pays for the design and build. What matters is what happens if you need to leave early. Look for a clear buyout price, not "call us to discuss". And check whether there's a money-back period at the start.

## How fast changes get made

Your business changes, so your site will need to change too. Ask how changes are requested and how long they take. "Through your account manager" can mean weeks. Two business days is a fair standard.

## Whether it's designed or assembled

Cheap AI builders can produce a basic site in minutes for $15 a month. That's fine for some businesses. If you're paying an agency, you should get a site designed for your business, not a template with your logo on it.

## Speed and accessibility

Many small business sites load slowly on phones and fail basic accessibility checks, which costs visitors and search rankings. Ask what speed score the site will have at launch.

## The short version

A good plan is clear about ownership, has a fair way out, makes changes quickly and gets you a site that's designed and fast. If a provider can't answer those questions in writing, keep looking.
