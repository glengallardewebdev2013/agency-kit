If you've asked around for a website in Cebu, you've probably had answers from ₱8,000 to well over ₱100,000, and a lot of "message us for a quote". Here's what the market looks like, and what to check before you pay anyone.

## The going rates

For a basic small business website in the Philippines, published guides put the price at about:

- **₱8,000 to ₱25,000** from a junior freelancer
- **₱25,000 to ₱60,000** from a senior freelancer
- **₱50,000 to ₱150,000** from an agency

Monthly maintenance usually runs ₱2,000 to ₱5,000 for basic care and ₱5,000 to ₱10,000 for more. ([Praferosa Webworks, 2026](https://www.praferosawebworks.com/blog/website-cost-philippines-2026/))

Budget package shops sell sites from under ₱10,000, often with a required monthly fee. Agencies start much higher and often ask for a deposit before work begins.

## What actually changes the price

- **Pages.** A one-page site costs less than a 12-page one with a page per service.
- **Design.** A template with your logo is cheaper than a design made for your business. Both are fine, as long as you know which one you're buying.
- **Features.** Online booking, payments by GCash, Maya or card, and a second language for foreign guests all add work.
- **Revisions.** Some providers bill each change. Ask how many rounds are included.

## Five questions to ask before you pay a deposit

1. **Can I see the site before I pay in full?** The most common dispute with freelancers is a deposit paid, then work that stops halfway. ([Respicio & Co.](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/freelancer-non-completion-of-work-in-the-philippines-client-rights-explained))
2. **Whose name is the domain in?** It should be yours.
3. **When do I get the files and logins?** You should get them the day you pay, not "after the contract ends".
4. **Is the monthly fee required?** Hosting has to be paid somehow, but you should be able to leave and take your site with you.
5. **Is the quote the same as the published price?** If there's no published price, ask for every fee in writing, including the domain renewal.

## Is a Facebook page enough?

For some businesses, for now, yes. But your Facebook page is rented: the rules, the reach and even access can change. A website on your own domain is something you own, and it's what customers find when they search Google or ask an AI assistant.
